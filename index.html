Rides like a snowboard. Moves like a skateboard.

The RipStik has just two wheels, and each one can spin all the way around. That's what lets it move with nothing but a twist.

Step on

Put one foot on each end of the board. Hold a wall or a friend's hand for your first few goes.

Twist

Swing your hips and shoulders side to side. The front and back twist on their own, and the board starts rolling.

Carve

Lean into turns to swoop, weave and spin. Once it clicks, you can cruise a long way without ever kicking off.

Ride smart, ride longer

  • Always wear a helmet. Knee pads, elbow pads and wrist guards are a great idea while learning.
  • Learn on flat, smooth ground away from cars, like an empty court or a quiet path.
  • Wear closed-toe shoes with grippy soles.
  • Younger riders should be watched by an adult.

Questions, answered

Is it hard to learn?

It takes a little practice. Most riders feel wobbly at first, then it suddenly clicks. Start next to a wall or rail for support, and keep your knees bent.

Do I need to push off with my foot?

Nope. Once you're rolling, twisting your hips and shoulders keeps you moving. Some people give a small push to get started, and that's fine too.

How is it different from a skateboard?

A skateboard has four wheels and one solid deck. The RipStik has two wheels and a deck in two halves that twist. It feels more like surfing or snowboarding.

Does it need putting together?

No. It comes fully assembled, so you can take it out of the box and ride.

Where should I ride it?

Smooth, flat surfaces are best: footpaths, driveways, basketball courts and skate parks. Avoid gravel, wet ground and busy roads.
